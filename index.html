The 6 Pipeline Steps in Detail

A step-by-step breakdown of how food travels across India's agricultural corridors.

Step 01

Step 1 Farm & Inputs

Seeds, fertilisers, pesticides, machinery and other inputs are deployed by farmers to grow crops or rear livestock, culminating in harvesting. Access to quality inputs determines baseline yields and sets the financial stage for rural households.

Step 02

Step 2 Aggregation & Procurement

Produce is collected from farmers through FPOs, cooperatives, mandis, traders and aggregators and consolidated into larger quantities. This phase bridges tiny farm-gate surpluses with commercial volume demands.

Step 03

Step 3 Storage & Logistics

Produce moves through warehouses, silos, cold storage and transportation networks to manage seasonality, spoilage and geographic distribution. Robust logistics protect perishable crops from rapid decay.

Step 04

Step 4 Processing & Packaging

Raw commodities are cleaned, graded, milled and converted into processed or value-added foods, then packaged and branded. This adds shelf-life and unlocks superior market value.

Step 05

Step 5 Distribution & Retail

Products move through distributors, wholesalers, supermarkets, kiranas, e-commerce and quick-commerce to reach consumers. This connects final retail demand with the broader supply network.

Step 06

Step 6 Consumption & Waste

Consumers buy food through retail or restaurants/food-service businesses; unsold and discarded food enters waste, recycling, animal-feed or compost streams, completing the life cycle.

Problems in the Indian Food Supply Chain

6 Key Points detailing structural breakdowns across agricultural touchpoints.

1. Fragmented Farming

Millions of small farmers have limited bargaining power, inconsistent quality and poor access to organised markets, creating inefficiencies at the source.

2. Poor Aggregation & Procurement

Multiple intermediaries, mandi dependence and fragmented procurement can increase costs while reducing the farmer's share of the final consumer price.

3. Storage & Cold-Chain Gaps

Insufficient warehouses, silos, refrigerated transport and cold storage lead to post-harvest losses, spoilage and price volatility, especially for perishables.

4. Inefficient Logistics

Long distances, fragmented trucking, multiple handling points and inadequate multimodal infrastructure increase transportation costs and delivery times.

5. Processing & Quality Issues

Low levels of food processing, inconsistent quality standards, contamination/adulteration risks and limited traceability reduce value addition and export competitiveness.

6. Distribution & Consumer Challenges

Complex distribution networks, inventory inefficiencies, food wastage and price fluctuations make it difficult to consistently deliver affordable, high-quality food to consumers.

Strategic Transformation

The Core Solution: Operational Scale Over Micro-Extraction

Fixing India's food supply chain requires re-engineering the architecture so fewer, more integrated entities handle wider chunks of the supply chain.

By focusing on end-to-end efficiency, cold-chain continuity, and digital transparency, the agricultural sector can eliminate predatory middleman extraction and secure higher returns for smallholders.
